## 📖 Executive Summary

Institutional autonomy is conventionally presumed to foster academic freedom, curricular agility, and reduced student distress. This study evaluates how autonomy manifests in the daily academic routines of undergraduate students across public, private, and national universities in Bangladesh.

Using a mixed-methods empirical survey ($N = 51$), the findings demonstrate a central **autonomy paradox**: autonomous institutions frequently leverage their independence to enforce accelerated semester calendars, compressed exam intervals, and intense grading policies, leading to elevated student stress and burnout rather than pedagogical flexibility.

---

## 📊 Key Survey Findings

* **The Autonomy Paradox:** **81%** of respondents agreed or strongly agreed that institutional autonomy in Bangladesh leads to increased academic pressure rather than freedom.
* **Health & Well-being Toll:** **85.7%** reported experiencing adverse mental or physical health consequences resulting directly from academic expectations.
* **Exam System & Anxiety:** **61.9%** noted that frequent, high-stakes exams drive chronic anxiety, while **58.6%** felt overwhelmed by overlapping or consecutive exam schedules.
* **Curriculum Relevance Deficit:** Only **20.7%** observed that their curricula were closely aligned with modern workplace demands.
* **Governance Asymmetry:** **43.1%** identified university administrations as maintaining strict centralized control over decisions, while only **13.7%** felt students had any influence.

## 📌 Primary Policy Recommendations

1. **Student-Centered Calendar Pacing:** Eliminate multi-exam clusters on identical or consecutive calendar days and allocate sufficient inter-semester breaks.
2. **Pedagogical vs. Bureaucratic Autonomy:** Re-channel university autonomy toward elective course diversity and active-learning methods rather than aggressive testing cadences.
3. **Institutional Mental Health Support:** Integrate dedicated on-campus psychological resources and counseling infrastructure to mitigate academic burnout.
4. **Inclusive Governance:** Establish formal feedback channels to include student representation in academic calendar revisions and workload policies.
